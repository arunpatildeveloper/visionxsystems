The Problem Space

Generative AI is making synthetic media increasingly difficult to distinguish from authentic content. Proving authenticity is now as hard as detecting fakery.

Digital Media Authenticity Research
Why This Project Exists

The problem is no longer just fake media.

Real media increasingly needs a way to prove that it is real.

Generative AI has made it easier than ever to create realistic images, videos and voices. The challenge is no longer only about detecting what is fake — it is increasingly about establishing confidence in what is genuine.

VisionX explores technologies that could help analyse these forms of media and understand whether signs of manipulation or synthetic generation are present.

01

Detect

Identify signs of manipulation or synthetic generation in digital media files.

02

Analyse

Examine visual, temporal, audio and metadata signals for forensic investigation.

03

Establish Trust

Explore frameworks for building confidence in the authenticity of digital content.

What VisionX Explores

Research Areas

01

Deepfake Detection

Not yet started

Exploring methods for identifying synthetic or manipulated facial media using computer vision and deep learning techniques applied to image and video sequences.

Planned approach: literature review, dataset selection, baseline model experiments.

02

Digital Media Forensics

Not yet started

Studying visual, temporal and metadata signals within digital files to understand origin, modification history and chain of custody of media artefacts.

Planned approach: metadata extraction tools, format analysis, forensic signal study.

03

Synthetic Media

Not yet started

Understanding how AI-generated images, video and audio can be recognised and differentiated from authentic, unmanipulated source content.

Planned approach: GAN output analysis, frequency-domain inspection, generative model study.

04

Digital Identity

Not yet started

Exploring impersonation, identity consistency and the growing challenge of synthetic identities used to deceive in digital environments.

Planned approach: face consistency checks, biometric signal analysis, identity-spoofing case study.

05

Cybersecurity

Not yet started

Studying how manipulated media can be used as a vector for fraud, social engineering, targeted deception and identity-based attacks.

Planned approach: threat modelling, attack surface analysis, social engineering case studies.

06

Digital Trust

Not yet started

Exploring how technology can help establish frameworks for verifying confidence in digital content and building trust in what we see online.

Planned approach: provenance tracking concepts, content authentication standards research.

Cybersecurity Intersection

Media authenticity is becoming a cybersecurity problem.

Traditional cybersecurity protects systems, networks and accounts. Modern threats increasingly involve manipulating what people see, hear and trust.

VisionX explores this emerging intersection — where digital media itself becomes the attack vector.

How the Research May Work

VisionX Research Framework

The following represents a planned research workflow. Implementation has not yet started.

01

Media Input

Planned

The system would accept image, video or audio files for examination. Defining supported formats, input constraints and preprocessing pipelines is part of early design work.

02

Signal Examination

Planned

Visual, temporal, metadata and audio characteristics would be extracted and examined. Understanding which signals are most meaningful for detecting manipulation is a core research question.

03

Model Analysis

Planned

Machine learning models would be applied to identify patterns associated with manipulation or synthetic generation. Model selection, training data and evaluation methodology are yet to be determined.

04

Interpretation

Planned

Signals would be combined into an output that could assist human investigation. How to present findings clearly and honestly — without false certainty — is a key design consideration.

About VisionX

VisionX is an academic final-year project exploring digital media authenticity and the growing challenge of synthetic and manipulated content.

The project brings together ideas from artificial intelligence, computer vision, digital forensics and cybersecurity to investigate how digital media may be analysed for signs of manipulation or synthetic generation.
